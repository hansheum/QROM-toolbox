\section{The Dual-Alphabet System of Notation}\label{sec:dual}
  \subsection{Dual Alphabets}
    We introduce the dual-alphabet notation, which allows us to reason about qubit
      states in both the computational and the Hadamard basis without the need to
      explicitly apply the Hadamard transformation every time.

     Recall the the Hadamard basis states $\ket +$ and $\ket -$. For 
      $b \in \{0,1\}$, define
      \[
        \ket{\hat{b}} \coloneqq \frac{\ket 0 + (-1)^b \ket 1}{\sqrt 2}\, ,
      \]
      i.e.\ $\ket{\hat{b}} = \ket +$ if $b=0$, and $\ket{\hat{b}} = \ket -$ if
      $b=1$. Thus, we are invited to think of the variable $\hat{b}$ as a bit
      defined over the alphabet $\{+,-\}$ in place of our usual alphabet $\{0,1\}$.

    In other words, we may identify the Hadamard basis states $\ket +$ and 
      $\ket -$ with the states $\ket{\hat{0}}$ and $\ket{\hat{1}}$. Notice how
      this generalizes to multiple qubits: Hadamard-transforming a
      computational basis state $\ket{x} = \ket{101 \ldots 01}$ is just 
      applying Hadamard gates to each qubit individually, so this yields the state
      $\ket{- + - \ldots + -} = \ket{\hat{1} \hat{0} \hat{1} \ldots \hat{0}
      \hat{1}} \eqqcolon \ket{\hat{x}}$, where we now have a bitstring 
      $\hat{x}$ defined over the dual alphabet, $\hat{x} \in \{+,-\}^n$.

    As you may know, the computational basis and Hadamard basis really are
      \emph{dual} bases (in the sense that they are mutually unbiased bases),
      and hence so are their alphabets: Writing out the state $\ket{\hat{x}}$ in
      terms of the computational basis, we have:
    \[
      \ket{\hat{x}} = 2^{-\nicefrac{n}{2}} \sum_{y} (-1)^{x \cdot
      y} \ket{y}\, .
    \]
    This identity also holds the other way around:
    \[
      \ket{x} = 2^{-\nicefrac{n}{2}} \sum_{y} (-1)^{x \cdot
      y} \ket{\hat{y}}\, .
    \]
    In particular, for the single-qubit states:
    \[
      \ket{b} = \frac{\ket{\hat{0}} + (-1)^b \ket{\hat{1}}}{\sqrt 2}
      = \frac{\ket{+} + (-1)^b \ket{-}}{\sqrt 2}\, .
    \]
    Being able to apply this translation on the fly, expressing any given state
    under whichever alphabet it is most simply expressed, will turn out to be very
    useful in simplifying intermediate calculations. As a bonus, it brings to
    the forefront many of the unique aspects that separate quantum algorithms
    from classical ones!

  \subsection{Dual-Alphabet Pseudocode}
    We have introduced the notation $\register[B] \xstore \register[A]$ for the
      operation that, if register $\register[A]$ is in state $\ket a$ and
      register $\register[B]$ is in state $\ket b$, applies the operation 
      \[
        \ket{a,b} \to \ket{a, b \xor a}\, .
      \]
      If $a$ and $b$ are bits, $a,b \in \{0,1\}$, then this is just a CNOT
      operation from register $\register[A]$ to register $\register[B]$.

    What is the equivalent operation for Hadamard states? Well, you may be aware
      that the $\Zgate$ gate plays the role of the $\Xgate$ gate in the Hadamard
      basis; in fact, $\Zgate$ and $\Xgate$ switch roles exactly when moving
      between bases:
      \begin{align*}
        \ket{0} \apply{\Xgate} \ket{1}\, ,\ \ket{1} \apply{\Xgate} \ket{0} 
          &\qquad \ket{0} \apply{\Zgate} \ket{0}\, ,\ \ket{1} \apply{\Zgate} -\ket{1} \\
        \ket{+} \apply{\Zgate} \ket{-}\, ,\ \ket{-} \apply{\Zgate} \ket{+}
          &\qquad \ket{+} \apply{\Xgate} \ket{+}\, ,\ \ket{-} \apply{\Xgate} -\ket{-}
      \end{align*}
      which in our notation may be written more succinctly as
      \begin{align*}
        \ket{b} \apply{\Xgate} \ket{b \xor 1}\, &,\quad \ket{b} \apply{\Zgate} (-1)^b \ket{b} \\
        \ket{\hat{b}} \apply{\Zgate} \ket{\hat{b} \xor \hat{1}}\, &,\quad \ket{\hat{b}} \apply{\Xgate} (-1)^b \ket{\hat{b}}
      \end{align*}
