\begin{abstract}
  The Random Oracle Model (ROM) has been highly successful in bringing to life
  cryptographic schemes that are 1) efficient and 2) tightly secure, with
  3) conceptually simple security proofs. 
  However, a quantum attacker would clearly be able to run the primitives our
  random oracles are modelling (such as SHA3) on a \emph{quantum
  superposition} of inputs.
  We can not exclude the existence of quantum attacks that employ such a
  strategy; hence, if we want to claim post-quantum security relative to a random oracle,
  we better ensure that our random oracle is \emph{quantum-accessible}.

  This is the Quantum Random Oracle Model (QROM).
  Unlike their classical counterparts, proofs of security in the QROM are often 
  1) conceptually challenging and 2) lossy. These issues boil down in large part
  to the non-applicability of three simple yet
  powerful techniques that sit at the heart of the classical random oracle
  model:

  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Programming: Altering the random oracle on certain inputs
    \item Simulation: Lazy-sampling a random oracle
    \item Extraction: Learning the inputs to the random oracle
  \end{enumerate}

  In this gentle introduction to provable security in the QROM, we will survey how
  tools developed over the past decade recover these capabilities, and how to
  apply them:

  \begin{enumerate}
    \item The one-way to hiding lemma (for reprogramming)
    \item The compressed-oracle technique (for simulation)
    \item Online-extractability of quantum random oracles (for extraction)
  \end{enumerate}

  Along the way, we will meet popular construction paradigms like the Fujisaki--Okamoto
  transformation and the Fiat--Shamir transformation, underlying the recent post-quantum
  standards ML-KEM (Kyber) and ML-DSA (Dilithium): Through examples
  lifted from their proofs of post-quantum security, we will show how
  and when to apply each tool, and the impact each will have on the overall
  tightness.

  We only assume knowledge of basic linear algebra; hence,
  this tutorial also serves to introduce both provable security and
  quantum physics, with ample examples and exercises throughout.   

  \vspace{\baselineskip}
  \textbf{Roadmap.}\par\smallskip\noindent
  \begin{description}[font=\ttfamily, labelwidth=3.2em, leftmargin=!, labelsep=1.2em, itemsep=0pt]
    \item[v0.1] Primers on provable security, the random oracle model, and quantum computing;
      the One-way To Hiding (O2H) lemma; OW-security to IND-security with the
      \U-transform  
    \item[v0.2] Quantum pseudocode notation; the compressed oracle technique
    \item[v0.3] Online extractability; CPA-to-CCA with the U-transform
    \item[v0.4] Adaptive reprogrammability via the GHHM resampling lemma, and the
      fixed-permutation O2H lemma; from NMA-secure to CMA-secure digital signatures
    \item[v1.0] The O2H Zoo: SC-O2H, DS-O2H, MRM, MRE, and how
      they compare in tightness and applicability
    \item[v2.0] Measure-and-reprogram; the Fiat-Shamir transformation
    \item[v3.0] Primer on quantum information theory; distance measures; how 
      the tools were derived
  \end{description}
