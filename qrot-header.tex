\begin{abstract}
  Proofs of security in the Quantum Random Oracle Model (QROM) face three main
    challenges, relating to the non-applicability of several conceptually simple yet
    powerful proof techniques that sit at the heart of its classical counterpart:

  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Recording: Or, how to learn from oracle queries.
    \item Reprogramming: Or, how to alter the output on certain inputs.
    \item Simulation: Or, how to efficiently implement a random oracle.
  \end{enumerate}

  \noindent
  In this gentle introduction to the QROM, we will show how to
    recover these capabilities. Our approach will be to take
    a simple encryption scheme due to Bellare and Rogaway~\cite{CCS:BelRog93}
    as our motivating example, and prove increasingly
    strong statements about it, as we move through the literature on quantum
    random oracles and enlargen our toolbox.
    
  This tutorial only assumes knowledge of basic linear algebra and standard
    game-hopping techniques (though some prior experience with the classical
    random oracle model doesn't hurt).

\end{abstract}
